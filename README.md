## Problem Title
SIH 25010: Smart Crop Advisory System for Small and Marginal Farmers
## Problem Description
A majority of small and marginal farmers in India rely on traditional knowledge, local shopkeepers, or guesswork for crop selection, pest control, and fertilizer use. They lack access to personalized, real-time advisory services that account for soil type, weather conditions, and crop history. This often leads to poor yield, excessive input costs, and environmental degradation due to overuse of chemicals. Language barriers, low digital literacy, and absence of localized tools further limit their access to modern agri-tech resources.

Impact / Why this problem needs to be solved

Helping small farmers make informed decisions can significantly increase productivity, reduce costs, and improve livelihoods. It also contributes to sustainable farming practices, food security, and environmental conservation. A smart advisory solution can empower farmers with scientific insights in their native language and reduce dependency on unreliable third-party advice.

Expected Outcomes

• A multilingual, AI-based mobile app or chatbot that provides real-time, location-specific crop advisory.
• Soil health recommendations and fertilizer guidance.
• Weather-based alerts and predictive insights.
• Pest/disease detection via image uploads.
• Market price tracking.
• Voice support for low-literate users.
• Feedback and usage data collection for continuous improvement.

Relevant Stakeholders / Beneficiaries

• Small and marginal farmers
• Agricultural extension officers
• Government agriculture departments
• NGOs and cooperatives
• Agri-tech startups

Supporting Data

• 86% of Indian farmers are small or marginal (NABARD Report, 2022).
• Studies show ICT-based advisories can increase crop yield by 20–30%.

## Proposed Solution
The proposed solution is a smart, AI-driven mobile application or chatbot designed specifically for smallholder farmers. It will provide hyper-localized, real-time advisory using data such as:

Soil type and health (based on surveys or test inputs)

Local weather forecasts and historical data

Crop cycles and land use history

Real-time pest and disease trends

Market price movements

Key Features:

Multilingual Interface: Available in major regional languages to ensure accessibility.

Image-Based Diagnosis: Farmers can upload photos of affected crops for AI-powered pest/disease identification.

Voice Assistance: Natural Language Processing (NLP)-enabled voice interaction for users with low literacy.

Smart Recommendations: Personalized inputs for crop selection, irrigation, pest control, and nutrient management.

Offline Functionality: Basic features available without internet; data syncs when connectivity is restored.

Market Linkages: Access to current mandi prices and digital marketplace connections.

Feedback Loop: Collects usage and impact data to refine recommendations over time.
## Technical Approach
Technologies to Be Used:

Mobile Development: React Native / Flutter for cross-platform mobile apps.

Backend Services: Python, Node.js with cloud support (AWS/GCP/Azure).

AI Models:

Image Recognition: TensorFlow / PyTorch for plant disease and pest detection.

Recommendation Engine: Machine learning models trained on agronomic datasets and historical outcomes.

NLP & Voice: Google Dialogflow or Microsoft Azure Bot Service with support for Indian languages.

APIs:

Weather data (e.g., OpenWeather, IMD APIs)

Market price data (Agmarknet API)

Government soil health and KCC databases

Methodology:

Farmer Onboarding: Via extension officers, FPOs, or village-level entrepreneurs (VLEs).

Data Input & Customization: Farmers provide basic crop and land data; app begins offering tailored insights.

Real-Time Advisory: Based on weather, soil, and disease trends.

Feedback Loop: Farmers rate advice or submit outcomes, feeding data back into the learning system.

Continuous Updates: AI models retrained periodically to adapt to local patterns and anomalies.
## Feasibility and Viability
Technology Availability: All core technologies are readily available and scalable.

Operational Feasibility: Leverages existing rural infrastructure (mobile phones, extension networks).

Pilot-Ready: MVP can be deployed in select districts in Punjab for proof of concept.

Potential Challenges:
Challenge	Strategy
Low digital literacy	Integrate voice and video-based guidance; conduct training workshops
Connectivity issues	Build offline-first features with lightweight data sync
Resistance to change	Involve local influencers, use participatory design methods
Data privacy & misuse	Ensure strong privacy policies and data encryption
